IMPORTANT STORY • 2026

India's Students Raise Their Voice for a Fair Examination System

What Happened?

In 2026, a major student-led movement emerged in India over alleged irregularities and leaks related to competitive examinations, including the NEET-UG medical entrance examination. NEET-UG is the national entrance examination used for undergraduate medical admissions. The controversy affected students who had spent months or years preparing for competitive examinations. Students demanded a fair examination system, proper investigation into leaks and irregularities, accountability and action against those responsible. The movement later became broader and focused on problems in India's examination system. When Did the Protest Become Bigger? The movement grew strongly during June and July 2026. Students organised demonstrations and sit-ins, particularly in Delhi. The protest became known as the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) movement. The movement included students and young people from different parts of India. Sonam Wangchuk's Hunger Strike A major part of the movement was Sonam Wangchuk's hunger strike. He began an indefinite hunger strike at Jantar Mantar, New Delhi, on 28 June 2026, in solidarity with the students and their demands. His fast continued for 26 days. He was later taken to hospital because of health concerns and ended the hunger strike on 23 July. 20 July — March Towards Parliament On 20 July 2026, more than 10,000 protesters marched towards Parliament in New Delhi. Police used tear gas and baton charges to disperse protesters. The incident attracted national and international attention. What Were Students Demanding? The main demands included: Fair and transparent examinations Investigation into alleged paper leaks Accountability for irregularities Protection of students' future Reforms in the examination system Action against those responsible What Happened Later? The protests continued to put pressure on the government. On 25 July 2026, Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan announced his resignation after weeks of youth-led protests. The movement then called off its protests, describing the resignation as a major victory.

Why Is It Important?

This story is not only about an examination. It is about students' future and trust in the education system. For a student, an examination can decide admission to a college, career opportunities and years of hard work. Therefore, even a small irregularity can have a very serious effect.

Important Points

  • The issue involved alleged examination leaks and irregularities.
  • NEET-UG was one of the major examinations connected to the controversy.
  • Student protests grew during June and July 2026.
  • Sonam Wangchuk began a hunger strike on 28 June.
  • More than 10,000 protesters marched towards Parliament on 20 July.
  • Wangchuk completed a 26-day hunger strike.
  • Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on 25 July.
